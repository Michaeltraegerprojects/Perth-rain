How to read these forecasts

Rain day. Each card covers 24 hours from 9am to 9am, Perth time, as the Bureau of Meteorology records daily rainfall. The date on the card is the day the window ends: “Sat 3 Oct” means rain from 9am Friday to 9am Saturday.

Chances. “Chance of at least 0.2 mm” is the probability the gauge records measurable rain (0.2 mm is one step of the gauge). Chances under 1% are shown as “<1%”: small, not impossible. For heavy rain the model has little data, so ≥ 5 mm and ≥ 10 mm chances are rough.

Amounts. The expected total averages over wet and dry outcomes. The median is the middle outcome, so a median of 0 mm means a dry day is more likely than a wet one, not that rain cannot fall. The likely range covers the middle 80% of outcomes.

Lead time. Hours from the weather-model run the forecast is based on to the start and end of the window. A forecast is only issued when every model run it needs had been published before the forecast was made.

Status. Verified: every model input was checked against the exact model run in the public archive. Unverified: at least one run could not be checked because it is not archived. Unavailable: the needed model runs had not been published yet, which is not a forecast of zero rain. Stale: the exported forecast is old; this site cannot refresh it.

Fallback model. When the preferred combination of models is not available yet, a simpler calibrated model that needs fewer inputs is used. The card says when this happens.
