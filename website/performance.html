How the forecasts scored

Each model was tested on the most recent 20% of days, which it never saw while being fitted or chosen. Every method below was scored on exactly the same gauge, rain-day windows and readings.

What the numbers mean

MAE: average size of the error in mm per rain day. Bias: forecast minus observed, so a positive bias means too much rain on average. RMSE weights big misses more.

Calibrated median and calibrated expected total are two different single-number summaries of the same forecast; each is scored separately. Raw rows are the weather models' own totals before calibration. Season-aware climatology forecasts the usual rain for that time of year at that gauge, using training data only.

Brier score measures rain/no-rain probabilities (event: at least 0.2 mm, inclusive). Raw models give amounts, not probabilities, so they have no Brier score.

Paired differences compare errors day by day on the same days, with a 95% interval from a block bootstrap (7-day blocks). If the interval includes zero, the difference is not significant and any ranking is descriptive only. Several comparisons are made, so one significant result on its own should not be over-read.

In short: the calibrated forecasts clearly beat climatology. Against the raw models and their blend, most rain-amount differences are within noise, so no general accuracy gain over the raw models is claimed.
